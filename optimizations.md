Idea (2026-11-01, goal-weighted-admission): score an OASIS candidate by its last-layer gradient's inner product with a declared probe gradient, instead of by gradient energy; status: untested.
Observation: OASIS ranks current examples by relative last-layer energy and redundancy ([OASIS summary](/Papers/OASIS-summary.md)); GREATS (NeurIPS 2024) beats GradNorm by 3.8 points on its nine-subject MMLU slice, and dual-weighted residuals weight a residual by a functional adjoint rather than by residual size (Govoeyi and Richter, arXiv:2604.01835).
Mechanism: keep the admission gate, replace the energy term with a ghost inner product against a fixed, test-disjoint retention probe plus a current-task probe, and leave SIREN redundancy unchanged so the substitution is one variable.
Example: a batch of four instructions in which two have large gradients on template tokens and one has a small gradient aligned with a held-out old-task probe; the aligned example should outrank the template-energy examples.
Test: matched admitted count and matched scoring FLOPs against OASIS energy on one stream at or below 700M; reject if retained semantic accuracy moves by under 1 point, or if a noise probe matches the real probe.
Risk / novelty: the probe can be a disguised validation leak, and Adam's normalizer can make the SGD ghost product the wrong direction; novelty unverified; GREATS and LESS already select by gradient alignment in static fine-tuning.

Idea (2026-11-01, demand-routed-share): set the replay-versus-current slot fraction by comparing current reducible loss with the rise from a cached commit-time loss, instead of TSR's fixed branch probability; status: untested.
Observation: the hybrid draws a replay branch at a configured probability ([combined pipeline](/design/combined-pipeline.md)); RoD (arXiv:2609.40089) lets adaptation and replay compete and, at matched legal adaptation, reports validation-loss forgetting of 0.071 versus 0.125 for a 10% mixture.
Mechanism: cache the scalar training loss of each retained row at memory commit, rescore it with the current learner, and let rows with a positive rise compete with current-batch scores for the same slots. Do not train RoD's adaptation specialist.
Example: after a task that leaves old losses flat, the cached rises are ~0 and the next update should be almost all current data; after a task that doubles two retained losses, those two rows should enter the batch.
Test: frontier against fixed probabilities {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1} at the same token budget, byte cap, and writer; reject if any fixed probability dominates the demand rule on both new-task and retained semantic accuracy across three seeds.
Risk / novelty: a cached scalar is not RoD's reference-model loss and can track template drift rather than forgetting; novelty unverified; RoD and RHO-LOSS already allocate by reducible loss when a reference model exists.

Idea (2026-11-01, layer-local-signature): replace TSR's single probe gradient with one cosine per layer, and do not route a layer whose within-task probe gradients change sign; status: untested.
Observation: TSR's own limitation is that an average signature can cancel opposing subskills ([TSR summary](/Papers/TSR-summary.md)); TreeLoRA (ICML 2025) routes from layer-wise gradient similarity, with only tenths-of-a-point vision gains over HiDe-LoRA.
Mechanism: compute the probe gradient separately on each LoRA group, drop groups with mixed signs from the teacher routing weight, and keep aL-SAR's freeze rule untouched.
Example: a task whose layer-0 probe agrees across items and whose layer-8 probe has two opposite clusters; layer 8 should receive no transfer weight from that task.
Test: matched probe FLOPs against the current single-vector TSR on the same stream; reject if retained semantic accuracy and routing entropy do not both move by a pre-registered margin, or if the mixed-sign layers were already unused by aL-SAR.
Risk / novelty: short task lists may not need a tree or a bandit, and sign cancellation in probe gradients may be template noise; novelty unverified; TreeLoRA, ASO-LoRA, and TSR already use gradient geometry to route.

Idea (2026-11-01, random-layer-control): before crediting aL-SAR's Fisher-per-cost prefix, unfreeze a random subset of layers with the same expected FLOPs and always leave the end layers trainable; status: untested.
Observation: aL-SAR freezes a prefix from Fisher information per cost ([aL-SAR summary](/Papers/aL-SAR-summary.md)); LISA (NeurIPS 2024) randomly samples middle layers and raises LLaMA-2-7B MT-Bench from LoRA 4.45 to 4.94 on a single fine-tune, which is not a forgetting result.
Mechanism: one control arm. No change to retrieval. The sampler draws the same expected number of trainable layers as the Fisher rule and holds the draw for the same number of steps.
Example: if the Fisher rule trains 4 of 16 layers on a batch, the control also trains 4 of 16, chosen without Fisher, for the same step block.
Test: same token budget and same unfrozen-FLOP counter; reject the Fisher rule if retained and new-task semantic accuracy both stay within 1 point of the random arm across three seeds.
Risk / novelty: a matched FLOP counter can lie if frozen layers still receive a full backward; this arm is a baseline, not a candidate method; novelty unverified; LISA is the published single-task version of the random schedule.

Idea (2026-11-01, sum-gradient-energy): rank an OASIS candidate by the squared norm of its summed response-token last-layer gradient, equal to the current mean-gradient energy times the square of the supervised-token count; status: untested.
Observation: admission squares the last-layer gradient of the per-example mean of response-token cross-entropy (`src/models/model_causal_lm.py` `_batched_head_gradients`, `src/pipelines/oasis.py` `informativeness`). That average's norm shrinks as the completion grows. LESS Table 13 (ICML 2024, arXiv:2402.04333) reports Llama-2-7B MMLU 47.3 at selected length 11.3 for the vanilla inner product, 50.2 at length 15.1 for cosine-normalized features, and 46.5 at length 105.4 for random.
Mechanism: keep SIREN's cosine and the admission rate. Score Equation 2 on the gradient of the summed token loss, which multiplies today's energy by n². The supervised count is already computed and discarded. Add no probe and no teacher pass. `goal-weighted-admission` still dots the length-biased mean gradient; this idea changes only the energy's length scale.
Example: two responses with the same per-token gradient direction, lengths 8 and 64. Today's energy ranks the short one higher whenever the token gradients fail to add perfectly. Summed energy ranks the long one higher when those gradients accumulate.
Test: one instruction stream, model at or below 700M, matched admission rate and matched last-layer scoring FLOPs, against current OASIS energy and against length-stratified random admission at the same rate. Reject if the admitted median response length does not move, if retained semantic accuracy and format validity both stay within 1 point of current OASIS, or if the stratified-random arm matches summed energy.
Risk / novelty: cosine-normalized LESS still won MMLU with a short subset, so removing the short bias can lower accuracy; prompt tokens are already masked here, and cosine-versus-dot-product is already LESS; novelty unverified.

Idea (2026-11-01, excess-token-energy): build the OASIS last-layer gradient from response tokens whose cross-entropy exceeds the batch median, then square that restricted gradient; status: untested.
Observation: Equation 2 squares the gradient of the mean over every response token ([OASIS summary](/Papers/OASIS-summary.md)). Rho-1 (arXiv:2404.07965) reports that 51% of tokens in its math run stay low-loss and that training on high excess-loss tokens beats full-token training by over 16 points on GSM8K and MATH for 1B and 7B models. `sum-gradient-energy` multiplies the diluted mean by n², which still includes the easy tokens.
Mechanism: keep SIREN, the admission rate, and the head backward. Zero the logit gradient on response tokens at or below the batch median loss before the average, and do not rescale by n². The token losses are already formed inside `_losses`. No reference model and no probe.
Example: a 64-token solution with 60 tokens the model already predicts and 4 it does not. The mean gradient is diluted by about 64. The restricted gradient is the average of those 4.
Test: one instruction stream, model at or below 700M, matched admission rate. Arms: current mean energy, this restricted energy, `sum-gradient-energy`, and a random token mask with the same keep rate. Reject if retained semantic accuracy stays within 1 point of the mean, if the random mask matches the median mask, or if token NLL falls by more than the pre-registered margin. Report semantic accuracy and token NLL separately.
Risk / novelty: Rho-1's persistently hard tokens are 11% of its census, so the worst tokens can be noise; a batch median is not excess over a curated reference, and Rho-1's self-reference gain was about 3.3 points rather than 16; novelty unverified.

Idea (2026-11-01, dual-replay-weight): replace TSR's fixed branch probability with a projected step on one replay multiplier, using a test-disjoint probe loss and a declared tolerance; status: untested.
Observation: `demand-routed-share` compares gradient energy with a rise in cached loss. Those are different units. PDCL (arXiv:2310.00154) sets the replay weight to a multiplier that increases only when a past-task loss exceeds a tolerance. MSSR (arXiv:2603.09892) already finds loss-triggered replay within about a point of a more elaborate scheduler on 7B–8B models.
Mechanism: keep CSReL as the writer and keep OASIS as the admission rule. Maintain one multiplier λ. After each update, add a step of (probe loss minus the tolerance) and project λ onto the nonnegative reals. Set the branch probability to λ / (1+λ). The probe is a fixed slice of retained training data, disjoint from the test set, scored with the same reduction as the trainer.
Example: a task that leaves the probe loss below the tolerance drives λ toward 0 and the next updates stay on current data. A task that pushes the probe loss above the tolerance raises λ and draws replay more often.
Test: the frontier against fixed probabilities {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1} and against a loss-threshold trigger, at the same token budget and byte cap. Reject if any of those arms matches or beats the dual rule on both new-task and retained semantic accuracy across three seeds.
Risk / novelty: PDCL's sensitivity bound grows with the square root of parameter dimension over buffer size and is not a guarantee at 700M; the tolerance can send λ to 0 or to infinity, as their Figure 4 shows; novelty unverified. Do not run this arm in the same experiment as `excess-token-energy`.

Idea (2026-11-05, era-generator-replay): sample replay from a retained task's frozen era teacher instead of replaying its stored gold response; status: untested.
Observation: the hybrid already persists one immutable era snapshot per task and uses it only for logits ([combined pipeline](/design/combined-pipeline.md)). SSR (ACL 2024, arXiv:2403.01244) synthesizes from the base model and refines with the latest checkpoint; OPR (arXiv:2605.29495) rolls out the latest checkpoint on stored prompts.
Mechanism: compare three levels of stored content at matched replay tokens: real prompt with gold response, real prompt with era-teacher response, and base-model prompt with era-teacher response from a few seed demonstrations. SSR §4 uses the base model for synthesis because few-shot ability degrades after fine-tuning. Keep CSReL as the writer over the generated candidates. Label each synthetic row with its generating checkpoint.
Example: after MeetingBank, its era adapter writes summaries for 50 retained meeting prompts. The next task replays those summaries, not the gold ones, and retained ROUGE-L is compared with gold replay of the same token count.
Test: one TRACE generation stream, model at or below 700M, three seeds, matched replay tokens and writer. Arms: random gold replay, OPR-style latest-checkpoint rollout, SSR-style base synthesis, and era-teacher rollout. Reject if era-teacher rollout does not beat both synthetic arms on retained output quality, or if random gold replay beats it at every budget.
Risk / novelty: a model under 700M can write fluent wrong text that replay then reinforces. An adapter can exceed the bytes of a small text memory, so the defensible benefit is not retaining source data, not smaller storage; novelty unverified; SSR and OPR own the general mechanism.

Idea (2026-11-05, output-drift-retrieval): prioritize retained prompts by how much the learner's generated output has moved from its era teacher's output, instead of by loss or exposure; status: untested.
Observation: aL-SAR retrieves by discounted exposure and class similarity ([aL-SAR summary](/Papers/aL-SAR-summary.md)), and `demand-routed-share` uses a cached loss rise. LESS Appendix J warns that cross-entropy and task accuracy need not move together on long generations. No retrieval score here looks at generated text.
Mechanism: at each task end, generate greedily on retained prompts from the learner and the era teacher, score the pair with a normalized token-level distance, and sample replay in proportion to that drift. Generation replaces the loss rescore. Charge its cost to selection.
Example: a retained summary prompt keeps a flat token NLL while the learner's output changes from a summary to a copied sentence. Loss-based retrieval ignores it and drift retrieval replays it.
Test: matched replay tokens against uniform retrieval, aL-SAR retrieval, and `demand-routed-share` on one generation stream at or below 700M, three seeds. Reject if retained output quality stays within 1 point of uniform, or if drift and token-NLL rise rank prompts almost identically.
Risk / novelty: harmless paraphrase counts as drift, and generation on every retained prompt may cost more than the replay it saves; novelty unverified; GKD and SDFT use on-policy signals in the objective rather than for retrieval.

Idea (2026-11-05, mode-coverage-writer): require the memory writer to keep every response mode of a task, and report output-mode collapse beside accuracy; status: untested.
Observation: in the [600-example pilot](/results/experiments/pilot-600-audit.md), CSReL banking memory fell to 3/15 intents before travel while random kept 14/15, and 89 of 150 initial banking predictions were two labels. CSReL's objective does not see response coverage ([CSReL summary](/Papers/CSReL-summary.md)).
Mechanism: partition retained candidates into response modes, using declared classes where they are genuine and clusters of frozen-model response embeddings otherwise. Run CSReL inside per-mode quotas. Report the effective number of modes in generated outputs per task.
Example: a 60-row banking memory takes 4 rows per intent and CSReL chooses within each intent, so routing cannot crowd out order_checks.
Test: same memory size and passes against CSReL, random, and stratified random across three seeds, with shuffled learner order. Reject if stratified random matches the writer, or if coverage rises without retained output quality rising.
Risk / novelty: on labeled tasks this may reduce to plain stratification, and response clusters on long text may track template rather than meaning; novelty unverified; Adapt-infinity already balances selection across pseudo-skill clusters.

Idea (2026-11-05, memory-as-context): spend the same retained memory as retrieved few-shot context at generation time, instead of as training data, and measure when each use wins; status: untested.
Observation: the writer's records are used only as replay batches ([combined pipeline](/design/combined-pipeline.md)), and `predict` receives no memory (`src/models/model_causal_lm.py` `predict`). "When Continual Learning Moves to Memory" (arXiv:2604.27003) argues that the bottleneck moves to retrieval under a limited context window; a search found no same-buffer comparison with fine-tuning replay.
Mechanism: fix memory M. Compare training on M, retrieving the top-k rows of M into the prompt by frozen-model embedding similarity, and both. Reuse the same CSReL membership in every arm so only the use of memory changes.
Example: with a 60-row memory, a retained banking query retrieves its 3 nearest rows into the prompt and the model takes no replay update for that task.
Test: same M, new-task updates, seeds, and evaluation prompts across arms, on one short-output and one long-output task at or below 700M. Report retained quality against generation tokens. Reject if retrieval never beats replay at any memory size, or if random-row context matches nearest-row context.
Risk / novelty: context raises every later inference cost and small models may copy demonstrations, while retrieval must work without a task label; novelty unverified; L2P is a vision precedent for query-keyed memory.

Idea (2026-11-05, era-guided-decoding): recover retained-task output at generation time by steering the learner's logits with a routed era adapter; status: untested.
Observation: every forgetting control in this project acts during training, but era adapters stay on disk and TSR already computes task similarity ([TSR summary](/Papers/TSR-summary.md)). LGCD (ICLR 2026) corrects uncertain adapted-model tokens with an approximate pretrained model, and CoLD (arXiv:2505.14620) contrasts a LoRA expert with its base.
Mechanism: route a prompt to one era without a task label, then decode from `learner + w * (era - learner)` only where the learner's top-token margin is low. With `w = 0` this is the current learner. Load the era adapter on demand under the existing residency rules.
Example: a banking query after the travel task routes to the banking adapter. The learner's confident tokens stay unchanged, and its uncertain intent token follows the banking era.
Test: zero and small replay budgets against the plain learner, hard routing to the era adapter, and an oracle-task-label arm, at or below 700M across three seeds. Reject if hard routing matches guidance, or if misrouted prompts lose more quality than routed prompts gain.
Risk / novelty: two forwards per token roughly double generation cost, and a wrong route can damage correct output; novelty unverified; LGCD and CoLD are close decoding-time neighbors, though neither was read in full here.

Idea (2026-11-05, rollout-distillation): evaluate era-teacher KL on prefixes generated by the learner, instead of only on gold prefixes; status: untested.
Observation: TSR-style distillation is computed over the collated batch's token positions ([models contract](/design/models.md)), so the learner is regularized on prefixes it may never produce during free-running generation. OPR positions SDFT, OPSD, and GKD as on-policy methods that change the objective.
Mechanism: on replay updates, roll out a short learner continuation without gradients, then compute the existing masked KL against the era teacher on that sequence. Keep cross-entropy on the gold row. Charge rollout tokens to the training budget.
Example: a learner that drifts into a wrong summary opening gets teacher guidance on that wrong prefix, which gold-prefix KD would never visit.
Test: matched updates and replay rows against gold-prefix KD, no KD, and OPR-style replay, on one generation stream at or below 700M across three seeds. Reject if retained output quality stays within 1 point of gold-prefix KD, or if the rollout cost exceeds a pre-registered training-time margin.
Risk / novelty: rollouts add training time, and a weak era teacher can transfer its own errors; novelty unverified; GKD already distills on policy-generated sequences outside this replay setting.

Idea (2026-11-05, noise-scheduled-replay): replay a retained row with a response whose final fraction t is regenerated by its era teacher, so t interpolates between gold replay and full regeneration; status: untested.
Observation: replay here uses only the stored gold response (`src/pipelines/memory_baselines.py` `build_memory`). OPR (arXiv:2605.29495) sits at the full-regeneration end and warns that confidence filtering can keep wrong answers. Nothing in this project studies the points between those endpoints.
Mechanism: as the forward step, remove the final fraction t of the gold response tokens. As the reverse step, let the era teacher complete the remaining prefix. t = 0 is gold replay and t = 1 is era-teacher rollout. This borrows a diffusion noise schedule; it is not a diffusion model and has no score-matching objective.
Example: with t = 0.5, a 52-word MeetingBank summary keeps its first 26 words and the era teacher writes the rest. The row, its token count, and its replay slot are unchanged.
Test: same memory rows, updates, and seeds on one generation stream at or below 700M, for t in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1}. Report retained ROUGE-L and token NLL separately. Reject if t = 0 matches or beats every other t, or if a fixed random-suffix control matches.
Risk / novelty: a causal model can only regenerate suffixes, so early tokens stay gold, and small models can write fluent wrong suffixes; novelty unverified; OPR and SSR cover full regeneration.

Idea (2026-11-05, variational-lora-prior): train a Gaussian posterior over LoRA factors, using the previous task's posterior as the KL prior, so uncertainty decides which adapter weights may move; status: untested.
Observation: `LoRALinear` holds point estimates (`src/models/model_causal_lm.py`), so nothing records which weights earlier tasks depended on. BLoB (arXiv:2406.11675) trains a Gaussian LoRA factor with the reparameterization trick for uncertainty on a single fine-tune, and one search found no sequential posterior-as-prior use with LoRA.
Mechanism: optimize a per-task ELBO, the expected response loss minus beta times KL(q_t || q_t-1), sampling factors by reparameterization during training and using the mean for scoring and evaluation. The KL is closed form. Low-variance factors become expensive to change. No separate model or decoder is added.
Example: after a MeetingBank task, adapter weights that summaries rely on have small variance, so a later Py150 task pays a high KL to move them and uses weights with larger variance.
Test: same stream, updates, and seeds at or below 700M. Arms: point LoRA, variational LoRA with a fixed standard-normal prior, and posterior-as-prior, each with zero and small replay, with a beta sweep on validation data. Reject if posterior-as-prior does not beat the fixed prior on retained ROUGE-L, or if learned variances are near-uniform.
Risk / novelty: mean-field variance can underestimate importance as in EWC-style methods, and doubled adapter size and beta tuning add cost; novelty unverified; variational continual learning (Nguyen et al., 2018) and BLoB supply the two ingredients.

Idea (2026-11-05, self-consistency-replay-filter): admit a generated replay response only when several independent samples from its era teacher agree, instead of trusting one rollout's confidence; status: untested.
Observation: OPR (arXiv:2605.29495) lists confidently wrong outputs as a limit of self-confidence scoring, and SSR (arXiv:2403.01244) warns that synthetic answers carry model errors. Generated replay can then reinforce those errors. `era-generator-replay` has no quality gate of its own.
Mechanism: sample N responses from the era teacher for one retained prompt with distinct seeds. Keep the prompt only if a majority agree, by normalized answer match on short outputs and by pairwise token overlap on long ones, and replay the response closest to that consensus. Rejected prompts fall back to gold or are dropped by a declared policy.
Example: five ScienceQA samples give answer B four times and C once. The row is admitted with a B response. A prompt whose samples all differ is rejected.
Test: matched replay tokens and writer at or below 700M across three seeds, against unfiltered generation, OPR-style confidence filtering, and a random filter with the same keep rate. Reject if retained quality stays within 1 point of the random filter, or if agreement does not predict correctness on held-out validation rows.
Risk / novelty: a small model can agree on the same wrong answer, N samples multiply generation cost, and long outputs have no natural vote; novelty unverified; self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) is an established inference method, used here as a replay gate.

Idea (2026-11-05, replay-memorisation-frontier): measure how replay budget and selection method change verbatim memorisation and membership leakage of retained rows, alongside retention; status: untested.
Observation: replay trains on a small retained set repeatedly, and the [design](/design/combined-pipeline.md) already logs replayed and retained exposure but no privacy consequence. A selector that keeps hard or atypical rows, as reducible loss may, could raise leakage for exactly those rows.
Mechanism: for each existing arm, score retained rows, non-retained training rows, and held-out rows by loss-based membership inference and by verbatim continuation from a response prefix. Add one exposure cap per row as a mitigation. Use the existing arms, records, and memory writers.
Example: at a 60-row memory, a row replayed 200 times should reproduce its response from a short prefix more often than a row seen once in the same task.
Test: same arms, stream, and seeds at or below 700M, with a budget sweep. Report membership-inference AUC and verbatim rate beside retained quality. Reject if leakage does not rise with exposure, or if CSReL, random, and the exposure cap are indistinguishable from each other on every leakage measure.
Risk / novelty: loss-based attacks are weak on short outputs, templated datasets cause verbatim matches without memorisation, and public benchmarks only proxy sensitive data; novelty unverified; memorisation and membership inference are established, and their dependence on replay selection here is unmeasured.

Idea (2026-11-05, reward-gated-admission): add a reward-model score to OASIS admission and to task-end candidates, so that selection sees response quality rather than gradient size alone; status: untested.
Observation: OASIS ranks by last-layer gradient energy and redundancy ([OASIS summary](/Papers/OASIS-summary.md)), which ignores whether a response is good. OPR's reward-filtered variant uses a task reward on generated rows, and TSR's distillation term already penalizes deviation from an era model (`src/models/model_causal_lm.py` `distillation_loss`).
Mechanism: train a scoring head on the pre-trained LLM using the same task's training rows, with gold responses against corrupted or mismatched ones. Multiply OASIS energy by a calibrated reward score before its threshold, and pass the same score to CSReL as a candidate filter. Keep TSR's KD term as the deviation penalty.
Example: two long responses have equal gradient energy, but one is a mismatched answer from another prompt. The reward gate rejects it and admits the matching answer.
Test: matched admission rate and matched scoring cost at or below 700M across three seeds, against current OASIS, reward-only admission, and a shuffled-score control. Reject if the shuffled control matches, or if retained and new-task quality stay within 1 point of current OASIS.
Risk / novelty: the corrupted pairs may teach the head to spot corruption rather than quality, the reward head itself can drift, and repeated scoring adds cost; novelty unverified; reward models are standard in RLHF, and OPR already filters generated replay by a task reward.

Idea (2026-11-05, reducible-answer-divergence): rank CSReL candidates by how much sampled-answer divergence the reference model removes, instead of by a teacher-forced loss difference; status: untested.
Observation: CSReL scores `loss(subset) - loss(reference)` on gold tokens ([CSReL summary](/Papers/CSReL-summary.md)), so it never sees generated outputs. ADG (arXiv:2604.10448) selects by dispersion and anisotropy of K sampled answers, one-shot and offline, and a summary of its limitations says high divergence can reflect prompt ambiguity.
Mechanism: keep CSReL's rounds and quotas. For each candidate prompt, sample K answers from the subset and reference models, compute ADG's `s = (1-l)D + l*I` for each, and score `G = s(subset) - s(reference)`. Prompts that stay diverse under both models score near zero, so ambiguity is discounted. Select within answer-embedding bins, as ADG's Eq. 8 does. Gold responses stay the replay targets.
Example: an open creative prompt has high dispersion under both models and scores low. A summary prompt with scattered subset-model answers and tight reference-model answers scores high.
Test: same memory, rounds, and seeds on a TRACE generation stream at or below 700M. Arms: loss-based CSReL, one-shot ADG, random, and bin-stratified random. Reject if retained ROUGE-L stays within 1 point of bin-stratified random, or if the ranking is nearly identical to loss-based CSReL.
Risk / novelty: sampling cost is high (600 rows, K=5, 3 rounds gives about 12,000 answers per task), one-word answers make dispersion degenerate, and a poorly fitted reference makes scores noisy; novelty unverified; neither ADG nor CSReL combines the two signals.

Idea (2026-11-05, routed-sequence-kl-replay): replace TSR's gold-prefix token KL with replay on answers sampled from the routed era teacher, and add measured drift to TSR's routing weight; status: untested.
Observation: TSR computes KL at teacher-forced gold contexts ([TSR summary](/Papers/TSR-summary.md)), and its similarity favors transfer rather than retention. Marek et al. (arXiv:2605.26097) show that next-token loss on samples from a reference model estimates `KL(reference || model)`, with a frozen reference and mostly one new task. OPR uses the latest model, and SERS (NeurIPS 2025) labels with the base and latest models and uses similarity only to set a regularizer weight.
Mechanism: on a replay update, draw task j from TSR's weights, sample answers from era teacher j on retained prompts, and minimize their negative log-likelihood. Estimate `drift_j` as the mean of `log p_teacher(y) - log p_learner(y)` on those samples, and route by `softmax((cos(g_j, g_t) + b*drift_j) / tau)`. Keep gold cross-entropy for the current task.
Example: a compatible task with no drift and a dissimilar task with high drift can receive similar replay shares, whereas plain TSR would favor only the compatible task.
Test: at least four tasks, same replay tokens, updates, and seeds at or below 700M. Arms: gold replay with token KD, latest-model replay, era replay without drift, era replay with drift, and shuffled drift. Reject if era replay does not beat latest-model replay on retained ROUGE-L, or if shuffled drift matches real drift.
Risk / novelty: era teachers can preserve early mistakes, the drift estimate is noisy on few samples, and sampling adds compute; novelty unverified; Marek et al., OPR, and SERS each cover part of this.

Idea (2026-11-05, mode-exposure-retrieval): run SAR's exposure balancing over answer modes, so it works on language data with no class labels; status: untested.
Observation: SAR's effective frequency needs class totals and class similarity ([aL-SAR summary](/Papers/aL-SAR-summary.md)), and the [design](/design/datasets.md) forbids inventing classes from task IDs, which blocks SAR on open generation tasks. The pilot's memory and predictions also concentrated on a few intents ([pilot audit](/results/experiments/pilot-600-audit.md)).
Mechanism: cluster answer embeddings from retained rows into modes using training data only. Treat a mode as SAR's group, with `c_hat_i = c_i + sum_m S(m, m_i) * C_m`, where `S` is the cosine between mode centroids, and keep `p_i` proportional to `exp(-c_hat_i / T)`. Reuse embeddings from `reducible-answer-divergence` when available. Keep aL freezing out of this arm.
Example: if MeetingBank memory holds many budget summaries and few voting summaries, the budget mode's exposure rises quickly, so voting rows become more likely.
Test: same memory and updates against uniform retrieval, SAR with genuine classes where they exist, and SAR with shuffled mode labels, across three seeds. Reject if shuffled labels match real modes, or if generated-answer diversity and retained ROUGE-L both stay unchanged.
Risk / novelty: clusters may capture format rather than meaning, and centroid cosine is not SAR's gradient similarity; novelty unverified; ADG and MASS also use clusters, but for one-shot selection, not exposure-based retrieval.

Idea (2026-11-05, forgotten-token-replay): compute replay loss only on response tokens the learner now predicts worse than the era teacher did, instead of on every token; status: untested.
Observation: replay averages over all response tokens, including ones never forgotten (`src/models/model_causal_lm.py` `_losses`). Rho-1 (arXiv:2404.07965) trains on tokens by excess loss over a reference, and ssToken (arXiv:2510.18250) replaces the reference with a history model and adds attention scoring, both in single-run training. `excess-token-energy` changes OASIS's score, not the replay loss.
Mechanism: for a replay row, compute per-token `d = loss_learner - loss_era_teacher`, using the teacher forward TSR already runs. Train on the top rho fraction of tokens with positive d, and optionally blend with ssToken's attention score.
Example: in a 60-token summary, the learner still predicts the format tokens but has lost 6 tokens carrying the decision. Those 6 tokens drive the replay gradient.
Test: same rows, updates, and seeds at or below 700M, against full-token replay, a random token mask with the same keep rate, and a mask using the base model as reference. Reject if retained ROUGE-L matches the random mask, or if the mask selects mainly punctuation and format tokens.
Risk / novelty: isolated token losses may not repair sequence behavior, and a stale teacher can mark legitimate improvements as forgetting; novelty unverified; Rho-1, BLADE, and ssToken do not address forgetting across tasks.

Idea (2026-11-05, restore-and-stop-token-replay): replace the fixed token mask in `forgotten-token-replay` with a hinge on a cached per-token baseline, and remove rows and suffixes with no active tokens; status: untested.
Observation: `forgotten-token-replay` runs a teacher forward on every batch and trains selected tokens even after they recover. A summary of ssToken notes that masked tokens still pass through the forward pass, while TIP (arXiv:2604.14084) reports up to 47% lower peak memory from token selection. See [Idea_Refined](/Idea_Refined.md).
Mechanism: when a saved copy writes an answer, store each token's surprise `b_k`. Train with `max(0, surprise_current - b_k - m)`, so a recovered token has zero loss. Every R updates, use a no-gradient pass to drop rows with no active tokens and cut each row after its last active token, since a causal model's later tokens cannot affect earlier predictions. Test deleting the writing adapter after answers are stored.
Example: a 60-token row whose last active token is at position 20 trains on 20 tokens. When its active tokens all recover, the row leaves the batch until a later refresh finds that it has slipped.
Test: same rows, updates, and seeds at or below 700M. Arms: full-token replay, a top-d mask with teacher forward, hinge only, hinge plus cuts, and a random mask with the same keep rate. Report retained quality, new-task quality, wall time, peak memory, and stored bytes. Reject if retention falls more than 1 point against the top-d mask without a clear compute saving, or if the random mask matches.
Risk / novelty: baselines on sampled text can be too low to reach, stale refresh lists can miss a slipped token, and deleting adapters removes later regeneration; novelty unverified; MSSR's loss trigger, GEM-style constraints, and `demand-routed-share` are close at row or batch level, and one search found no token-level version.

Idea (2026-11-05, repair-gain-token-selection): rank a forgotten token by how much repairing it lets the current model regenerate the rest of its past self's answer; status: untested.
Observation: `forgotten-token-replay` scores tokens separately at correct prefixes, so it cannot see one wrong token derail free-running generation. CFT (arXiv:2510.10974) substitutes tokens and needs a correct final answer on one model, Critical Tokens Matter (arXiv:2411.19943) resamples from token positions on one model, and RGD-R (arXiv:2411.11932) shows partial rationales can restore a forgetting model but allocates replay per task. See [Idea_Refined](/Idea_Refined.md).
Mechanism: for the top candidates by `d_k`, generate greedily from the prefix before k and from the prefix including the saved token. Let `g_k` be the gain in similarity to the saved answer after k, set `g_k` to zero below a measured replica noise floor, and rank by `S_k = d_k * (1 + beta * g_k)`. No verifier or gold answer is needed.
Example: for a saved answer "The council approved the budget on Monday", repairing "approved" restores the rest, while repairing the paraphrase "council" changes nothing. So "approved" rises above "council" even though its `d` is lower.
Test: same rows, updates, and seeds at or below 700M on long-output tasks. Arms: top-d selection, 3B with real gains, 3B with shuffled gains, and CFT-style selection on verifiable tasks. Reject if shuffled gains match real gains, if retained quality stays within 1 point of top-d selection, or if most gains are within the noise floor.
Risk / novelty: 60 rows with 5 candidates and 2 rollouts cost 600 rollouts per refresh, teacher-forced token repair may not transfer to free-running repair after training, and isolated facts get no gain; novelty unverified; Matteson (arXiv:2606.15621) reports 9–23% replica disagreement in related counterfactual scoring, so a noise floor is required.

Idea (2026-11-05, repair-and-stop-token-replay): merge `restore-and-stop-token-replay` and `repair-gain-token-selection` into one replay rule with a generation check on each row, gain-ranked and gain-weighted tokens, and a stored-baseline stop; status: untested.
Observation: repair tests are costly when run on every row, and token-level stopping cannot see a sentence that still derails. Each parent idea addresses the other's weakness. Full design: [Idea_Refined](/Idea_Refined.md).
Mechanism: at each refresh, skip rows whose generated answer already matches the stored saved-copy answer. On failing rows, measure repair gains for the top forgotten tokens and rank them by `S_k = d_k * (1 + beta * g_k)`. Train only until each active token recovers, using `w_k * max(0, s_k - b_k - m)` with `w_k = 1 + beta * g_k`, and cut each row after its last active token. Feed mean `s_k - b_k` to routing. If no row needs replay, use the update for the current task.
Example: for a saved answer "The council approved the budget on Monday", "approved" ranks first and trains at higher weight. Once the model regenerates that answer, the row stops using compute until a later refresh shows it has slipped.
Test: same memory and seeds at or below 700M, compared at matched updates and at matched wall time. Arms: full-token replay, random mask, top-d mask, top-d with stopping, gains with no stopping, the full method, the full method without the row check, and shuffled gains. Reject if a random mask or shuffled gains match it, or if the row check skips rows that the full-token arm shows have forgotten facts.
Risk / novelty: a similarity check can pass a sentence with one wrong fact, a skipped row can slip between refreshes, and saved-copy mistakes get reinforced; novelty unverified; CFT, Critical Tokens Matter, RGD-R, and MSSR each cover part of this.

Idea (2026-11-06, tsr-sized-adapter-growth): set each topic's extra LoRA rank from its TSR similarity to retained topics, instead of using one fixed rank; status: untested.
Observation: `LoRALinear` uses one fixed rank for every topic (`src/models/model_causal_lm.py`), although TSR already measures how different a topic is before training ([TSR summary](/Papers/TSR-summary.md)). CoDe-LoRA (arXiv:2610.08312) gives each task an expert of fixed rank. See [Idea_Refined_3](/Idea_Refined_3.md).
Mechanism: at topic start, add `r_min + (r_max - r_min) * (1 - max cos(g_j, g_t))` new adapter directions with zero-initial B, so predictions do not change before training. Keep TSR's probe separate so fingerprints remain comparable. Pair with `generation-tested-adapter-shrink` so the adapter does not grow without limit.
Example: with bounds 4 and 16, a topic at similarity 0.9 adds rank 5 and a topic at 0.1 adds rank 15.
Test: same stream, memory, updates, and seeds at or below 700M. Arms: fixed rank with the same mean size, this rule, and shuffled similarities. Reject if the shuffled rule matches this rule on retained and new-topic quality.
Risk / novelty: one mean fingerprint can be noisy on short probes, and similar topics can still need room; novelty unverified; expansion methods add adapters per task, but one search found none sizing them from a TSR-style signature.

Idea (2026-11-06, generation-tested-adapter-shrink): at topic end, shrink the adapter to the smallest SVD rank that can still generate retained answers, instead of choosing directions by energy or base-model similarity; status: untested.
Observation: SLoRA (ACL 2026) removes low-similarity directions using base weights and no data, and Merge before Forget (arXiv:2512.23017) keeps a fixed-size merged adapter. Neither checks generated answers on retained questions. The recipe already has an answer check and a CSReL memory ([repair and stop](/design/repair-stop.md)). See [Idea_Refined_3](/Idea_Refined_3.md).
Mechanism: compute each adapter's SVD, build a few candidate ranks, and run the answer check on retained questions plus a quality check on current-topic rows for each. Keep the smallest passing rank, and only then save the era copy and its baselines. Use training data only, never held-out data.
Example: if ranks 24, 16, and 12 pass within a 2-point tolerance and rank 8 falls well below it, keep rank 12.
Test: same final size against strongest-directions-only, base-subspace filtering, and no shrinking, across three seeds at or below 700M. Reject if strongest-directions-only matches it on held-out retained quality, or if it passes retained rows while failing held-out old questions.
Risk / novelty: a global rank choice can hide damage in one layer, small memories can overfit the check, and each candidate costs generations; novelty unverified; SLoRA, Merge before Forget, and CoDe-LoRA address adapter consolidation without this generation test.

Idea (2026-11-06, fingerprint-layer-plasticity): lower a layer's learning rate where its TSR gradient conflicts with retained topics; status: untested.
Observation: TSR averages one signature over all adapter layers, and its summary says that average can hide opposing subskills ([TSR summary](/Papers/TSR-summary.md)). `layer-local-signature` uses per-layer signs for teacher routing, and TreeLoRA (ICML 2025) uses them to choose adapters. Neither sets per-layer learning rates here. OASIS and aL-SAR stay out.
Mechanism: split the existing fingerprint by layer group. Compute `a_l`, the routing-weighted cosine with retained topics, and set `lr_l = lr * clip(1 + gamma * min(a_l, 0), floor, 1)` once per topic. This uses no Fisher score and freezes no prefix.
Example: with gamma 1 and floor 0.25, a layer at agreement -0.5 trains at half rate, and a layer at +0.4 trains at the full rate.
Test: same stream and seeds at or below 700M. Arms: equal rates, this rule, shuffled layer agreement, and a random layer subset slowed by the same amount. Reject if shuffled agreement or the random subset matches this rule on retained quality.
Risk / novelty: per-layer gradients from few probes are noisier than the mean, and slowing conflicting layers can hurt new-topic learning; novelty unverified; TreeLoRA and ASO-LoRA are the closest uses of layer-wise gradient geometry.

Idea (2026-11-06, zero-start-growth-layer): add a gated decoder layer whose gate starts at zero as extra learner capacity, and test it against a higher-rank adapter of equal size; status: untested.
Observation: the learner can gain capacity only through rank today. LLaMA-Adapter (arXiv:2303.16199) shows a zero-start gate can add parameters without changing initial behaviour, though it adds prompts, not whole layers. See [Idea_Refined_3](/Idea_Refined_3.md).
Mechanism: after the final decoder layer, add `x + g * block(x)` with g starting at zero. TSR's probe excludes the block, and saved copies store it. Keep the total under 700M.
Example: one block on SmolLM2 adds 3,540,096 parameters, so the control is a rank giving the same parameter count.
Test: same stream and seeds against the matched-parameter adapter and against no growth. Reject if the matched adapter ties it on retained and new-topic quality, or if the gate stays near zero.
Risk / novelty: a shared new layer can itself forget and adds saved-copy bytes; novelty unverified; progressive and expansion methods are close relatives.

Idea (2026-11-08, fight-located-growth): move a topic's extra adapter rank, at a fixed total, towards layers where its TSR gradient conflicts with retained topics, and slow the old rank there; status: untested.
Observation: `tsr-sized-adapter-growth` adds the same extra rank to every layer, and `fingerprint-layer-plasticity` slows conflicting layers, which may block learning exactly where the new topic needs room. TreeLoRA (arXiv:2506.10355) uses layer-wise gradients to share adapters, and LayRA (arXiv:2509.11414) adapts early and final layers for new languages. See [Idea_Refined_3](/Idea_Refined_3.md), step E.
Mechanism: take `a_l` from `fingerprint-layer-plasticity`, set `w_l = 1 - max(a_l, 0)`, and give layer l `extra_l = R * w_l / sum(w)`, where R is the total extra rank from `tsr-sized-adapter-growth`; new rank starts at zero B and trains at full rate, and old rank uses the plasticity rate.
Example: Py150 after MeetingBank on SmolLM2 with 15 extra rank (450 layer-ranks) and band weights 1.0, 0.4, 1.0 gets ranks 19, 7, 19 instead of 15 everywhere, at the same 4,579,200 parameters.
Test: the four-topic TRACE stream at or below 700M, 3 seeds, matched total parameters, against uniform growth with slowing and the same extra rank on random layers. Reject if random layers match it on retained quality and new-topic quality.
Risk / novelty: per-layer cosines from few probe rows are noisy, and moving rank changes which layers the saved copies store; novelty unverified; TreeLoRA, GainLoRA, and LayRA are the closest relatives.

Idea (2026-11-08, clash-ranked-block-choice): choose which LoRA blocks may train for a new language from per-block agreement between old-language and new-language probe gradients, instead of from validation trials; status: untested.
Observation: the English-to-Hindi summarization plan allows optional short validation trials to choose trainable blocks, whose cost must be counted ([Idea_Refined_4](/Idea_Refined_4.md), steps C and E). TSR's fixed zero-start probe already gives per-layer gradients ([TSR summary](/Papers/TSR-summary.md)), and `fingerprint-layer-plasticity` and `fight-located-growth` use them for rates and rank.
Mechanism: with the probe on mT5-small, compute `agreement(block) = cos(g_en, g_hi)` per block from a few rows of each language, then train only the most-agreeing blocks at a fixed parameter budget, or freeze the shared adapters in the most-clashing blocks and add zero-start rank there for the new language.
Example: at a budget of 638,976 parameters (rank 16 on two encoder and two decoder blocks), compare the four most-agreeing blocks, the four most-clashing blocks, late-only, and early plus late.
Test: three seeds from one English checkpoint, same random replay and Hindi exposure. Reject if the most-clashing and most-agreeing arms forget English equally on generated held-out summaries, or if randomly chosen blocks at the same budget match the agreement choice.
Risk / novelty: probe gradients from few rows may not predict forgetting under replay, and encoder and decoder blocks differ in size, so matching must be by parameters; novelty unverified; LayRA and TreeLoRA are the closest relatives.
